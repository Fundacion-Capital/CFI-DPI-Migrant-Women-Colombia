## 4. Review sequence and evidence retrieval plan

1. **Establish mandate (R01).** Identify the competent institutional body/role and its scope before assuming a conclusion will cover the project. A referral can be the appropriate outcome; no institution is contacted here.
2. **Reconcile history, consent and stewardship (R02-R07), alongside rights (R10).** The institution identifies what records it needs and which custodian may lawfully supply them. Use restricted evidence channels rather than copying participant records into the public repository. No participant load, recording review, live security check or exposure investigation is authorized by this packet.
3. **Assess the proposed derivative and access route (R08-R09).** This follows the consent, stewardship, privacy-history and rights determinations. Define exact versions, fields, outputs, residual risks and reuse terms before any separately authorized technical assessment. Open access is an objective, not a preselected outcome.
4. **Resolve approvals and reporting (R11-R12).** Separate CFI's approval before initial journal submission, both authors' package approval, applicable institutional conditions and the journal's eventual policy requirements. A new version may require renewed review. No submission or approval request is transmitted now.

For evidence not currently in hand, the appropriate owner should later supply either an authoritative record or an explicit limitation and its consequence. A missing file must not be replaced by an invented date, script, signature, exemption, delegation or waiver. This is an evidence-request plan only; no requests have been sent.

The register's dependency graph is a preparation aid, not an execution schedule. It permits preparatory parallel review of independent issues but grants no permission for additional actions or agent delegation.

## 5. Decision-return requirements

For each issue, an eventual reviewer should identify:

- Name, capacity and evidence of authority; exact study/material and use covered.
- Decision date/reference, reviewed evidence locators and versions, and the limitations of those records.
- A reasoned supported, conditional, insufficient-evidence, not-permitted or outside-remit outcome, separately for each relevant use.
- Any permitted/prohibited uses, conditions, evidence still needed and responsibility for satisfying each condition.
- What later action, if any, could be considered; a general institutional response is not a runtime, participant-access or release grant.

All fields are blank now. A future response must be authenticated and checked against its scope before any separate, authorized adoption into the formal dossier. Local JSON completion is not proof of institutional authority. No field in this preparation packet automatically updates `response_forms.json`, readiness or workflow state.

## 6. Options that remain for institutional determination

The institution may determine that the existing basis supports specified uses, impose conditions, require additional evidence/consent, limit access or prohibit a proposed use, or refer matters outside its remit. This packet does not recommend exemption, declare re-consent mandatory/unnecessary, or imply that a new form would repair the historical record.

If a new consent information sheet/addendum is required, it must be honestly dated/versioned and identified as prospective. Drafting is not consent, and recontact needs separate authority. A revised form must never replace the original and be represented as historically administered.

If unrestricted respondent-level release is not supported, an appropriately authorized alternative might involve controlled access or public code/documentation with an accurate restricted-data statement. Whether any alternative is acceptable to the eventual journal is not decided here. No data, synthetic records, transformations, licence or repository configuration are generated or changed.

Survey confirmations cannot establish permissions for migrant qualitative work, expert interviews, identifiable quotations, recordings or images. Those require separate source-specific review with the responsible coauthor/institution; the qualitative analysis itself is not modified.
