### Why this is compatible with serious reproducibility work

There is an important distinction between **preserving an exactly reproducible analysis** and **making every underlying participant record publicly downloadable**.

Current Elsevier guidance includes policy routes under which authors explain why data cannot be shared, although requirements vary by journal. That supports preparing an accurate data-availability statement—not promising that every outlet will accept restricted data. [Elsevier research-data guidelines](https://www.elsevier.com/en-in/researcher/author/tools-and-resources/research-data/data-guidelines).

As a stricter disciplinary example, the AEA’s current policy accommodates valid constraints on public data sharing while requiring public code, documentation and preservation commitments; exceptions and arrangements are subject to editorial assessment. Its guidance also distinguishes confidential inputs from the public replication archive. **This is evidence that restricted-data replication is a recognized route, not a guarantee of acceptance for our paper.** [AEA data and code policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/data/data-code-policy), [AEA replication guidance](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/data/faq).
