## 2. What the historical IVS actually measures

Higher historical IVS means greater **assumed vulnerability**, combining three equally weighted components when all are present:

| Component | Instrument and historical construction | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Migration tenure | `q2_1`: year first living in Colombia, integer constraint 1950–2026. `years_in_col = 2026 - q2_1` only when the year is below 2026. Reverse min–max scaling assigns 1 to the shortest and 0 to the longest observed tenure. | Tenure is not documentation, legal status, social integration or a measured causal protection mechanism. The scale depends on the construction sample's extrema. |
| Education | `q2_2`: ten ordered levels, mapped to **1.00, 0.89, 0.78, 0.67, 0.56, 0.44, 0.33, 0.22, 0.11, 0.00**. The choices explicitly include incomplete educational levels. | This rounded ordinal gradient is a scoring assumption, not a validated cardinal measure of human capital or recognized qualifications. |
| Occupation | `q2_3`: employer/patron and employee/worker → 0; independent worker, paid domestic worker, retired/pensioner and Other → 0.5; student, unpaid family worker and currently not working → 1. | The question measures main occupation, not contract formality, job security, wage adequacy, social insurance or occupational stability. These interpretations require independent evidence. |

All three questions are required in the **Caracterizacion** group, whose relevance is `${eligible_flag} = 1`; none adds its own item-level relevance. The consent-relevant eligibility calculation and ancestry were explicitly checked. Their own item properties and two choice lists do not change across these five definitions. **The enclosing group's presentation does change:** `field-list` in the earliest two versions, blank in the other three; the group relevance is unchanged. A separate reviewer identified this qualification, and fresh Stata assertions verify all five group rows. ## 3. Findings and correction candidates

**IVS-01 — four observed precision-dependent categories.** The written inclusive cutoffs are low ≤ 0.33, medium > 0.33 and ≤ 0.66, and high > 0.66. Comparing a float-stored score with double literals puts **one nominal 0.33 observation in medium and three nominal 0.66 observations in high**. The independent read-back asserts all four discrepancies. “Nominal” here is calculated from the unrounded tenure ratio and the declared decimal education/occupation points, not from the displayed score. Synthetic controls also show that other component combinations with the same nominal cutoff can round down and retain the expected category. A repair must specify and test numerical precision; indiscriminately rounding every score to two decimals would be a different operation. **No repair was applied.**

**IVS-02 — occupation and vulnerability claims exceed the item in places.** The legacy framework describes occupation as labor-market insertion; B00477 calls the component occupational stability, and B00473 links independent work to informality. The fielded question and its choices do not establish contract formality or stability. The three-point occupational ordering, equal component weights, interval interpretation of education, inverse-tenure assumption and vulnerability cutoffs need a substantive rationale. Historical code agreement cannot supply that rationale. This is a measurement/narrative-review issue, not authority to delete an item, choose new weights or change the conclusions now.

**IVS-03 — missingness and legal arrival-year edges are latent, not observed failures.** `rowmean()` uses available components; all 423 actual scores use three, but synthetic partial scores reweight the remaining components. The receipt named `partial_components` counts fewer than three components, including all-missing rows; both that count and the separately recorded all-missing count are zero here. A synthetic all-missing score is assigned high because the last comparison does not exclude Stata missing values. A legal instrument response of **2026** produces missing tenure under the strict `< 2026` condition, and a constant-tenure sample produces a zero normalization denominator. None of these conditions occurs in the selected coded sample. The hard-coded reference year and these edge rules require explicit treatment before reuse. They do not justify claiming that actual historical scores are missing or corrupted. Stata documents the available-component behavior in its [official egen manual](https://www.stata.com/manuals/degen.pdf).

**IVS-04 — initial stale-return suspicion not confirmed on this runtime.** The normalization uses `r(min)` and `r(max)` rather than the generated `max_years` variable. However, the installed `_gmax.ado` (**4.1.0, 15 June 2020**) internally calls `summarize …, meanonly` for this ungrouped, no-`missing` call. Three controls—cleared returns, an unrelated preceding summary and a preceding recode—all refresh the correct extrema, and the complete historical replay agrees exactly. The hypothesis that this formula used stale extrema in the inspected state is therefore **rejected**. Explicitly computing and retaining the intended extrema is still a reasonable portability/clarity candidate, subject to identity and zero-range tests; it is not a demonstrated repair of wrongly stored tenure. The local implementation is hash-pinned. Neither this observation nor the [official egen description](https://www.stata.com/manuals/degen.pdf) certifies every historical Stata/package runtime.
