# Quantitative methods: academic working draft

Editorial status, 5 November 2026: source-grounded draft for the academic track.
Recruitment statements distinguish the author's confirmations from retained
documentary/aggregate evidence. Academic estimation and profiling paragraphs
describe conditional plans, not analyses newly performed. Historical reference
counts are identified as such. This file has not been inserted into main.tex,
compiled, approved for submission or reconciled against newly executed results.

## Study design and quantitative contribution

The study combines a community-based survey with the existing qualitative,
institutional and comparative components to examine migrant women's engagement
with digital payments and remittances in Colombia. The quantitative strand
addresses three connected questions: how engagement and associated experiences
are distributed in the recruited cohort; how engagement covaries with selected
access, reported capability and administrative conditions; and, conditionally,
whether a coherent latent-response model supplies useful additional profiles.
The purpose is not to estimate a causal effect of Digital Public Infrastructure
(DPI), migration or a Fundación Capital program. Survey reports of digital
financial activity do not independently identify the infrastructure or payment
rail used in each transaction.

The full legacy manuscript supplies the research foundation and preserves the
historical analytical sequence. The academic revision distinguishes that history
from accepted changes to measurement, outcome priorities and interpretation.
Descriptive evidence, association analysis, conditional multivariable models and
optional profiling remain analytically separate. A technically reproduced
historical result is not automatically suitable for an academic claim, and a
revised definition is not presented as a mere relabeling of the old result.

## Population, recruitment and administration

The accepted quantitative population is adult women with international migration
experience living in the recruited urban and peri-urban communities, including
women who moved to Colombia from another country and Colombian women who lived
abroad and returned. Internal displacement alone did not qualify under the
author-confirmed recruitment account. The intended residence requirement was
approximately three months following arrival or return. Prior participation in
Fundación Capital programs, ownership of a personal telephone and the ability
to read or write were not participation requirements. These population statements
describe the intended and author-confirmed scope; they do not constitute a new
record-by-record eligibility certification.

Fundación Capital recruited a fixed team of migrant women leaders and former
program participants through existing relationships, recommendations and
invitations. Leadership and experience coordinating local migrant activities
were relevant to their selection as enumerators. Trained leader-enumerators
approached women they knew and interviewed additional women reached through
respondent referrals. Referred women did not become interviewers simply by
making referrals. The resulting procedure is described as non-probability,
community-based snowball or chain-referral recruitment, not a formal
respondent-driven probability design. Referral links and recruitment-network
structure were not systematically retained; no network-derived sampling weights
are constructed.

The initial target was approximately 20 interviews per enumerator. Some completed
more to compensate for shortfalls elsewhere. The author reports instructions
to obtain heterogeneity in characteristics such as age and employment, communicated
through training, a distribution table and continuing coordination. The detailed
allocation table is not available in the project folders. Descriptive distributions
can demonstrate the characteristics observed in the achieved cohort, but cannot
reconstruct numerical recruitment quotas, establish quota compliance or show
proportional population representation. Older women and recent arrivals were
reportedly harder to recruit; the extent of resulting undercoverage has not
been quantified. Enumerators were paid for their work; respondents received no
payment or reimbursement according to the author-confirmed account.

Interviews were assisted and conducted in Spanish, using telephone and in-person
administration according to availability. Telephone interviews reportedly
predominated. Mode was tracked separately rather than in a SurveyCTO item; no
verified numerical mode breakdown is retained in this package. Consequently,
neither telephone-only administration nor a mode-specific adjustment is assumed.
Prior aggregate source checks locate recorded interview dates between 9 December
2025 and 17 February 2026. Recorded interview dates are distinct from a formal
administrative closure date, and the raw-versus-coded timestamp discrepancy
remains an explicit provenance limitation.

## Consent, screening and administrative checks

Enumerators explained the study and recorded affirmative agreement in SurveyCTO
before substantive quantitative questions. The author's later corrected consent
account states that quantitative consent was documented only through the
SurveyCTO field: there were no separate quantitative paper forms or consent audio
recordings. Preserved signed forms concern the qualitative interviews and focus
groups. This correction supersedes the older population account's reference to
signed forms for in-person quantitative interviews. Participation was reported
as voluntary and unrelated to access to programs or benefits.

The five retained instrument definitions encode affirmative consent, the woman
response, age at least 18 and remittance receipt by the respondent or someone
in her household during the previous 12 months. The programmed calculation does
not itself establish every intended migration/residence condition. In particular,
first-arrival year does not prove three months of continuous residence or a
five-year migration cutoff. Screening answers were self-reported; enumerators
did not request official documentation. The author confirms that interviews
were closed when the programmed screen indicated ineligibility.

The research team reports that coordinators compared names and telephone numbers
across enumerators and cities to prevent repeated participation. This was an
administrative procedure involving restricted identifiers, not a new analytic
deduplication performed here. The one-per-household instruction was not supported
by systematically recorded household links. Residual within-network or possible
household dependence cannot therefore be ruled out from that instruction alone.
No participant records, consent payloads or administrative lists were inspected
for this source-only package.

Final journal-facing ethics and data-use statements must reflect the retained
evidence and institutional conclusion. This draft does not claim external
university/committee approval, exemption, a separate written public-sharing
choice or final release clearance that the records do not establish. The
reported spoken public-sharing explanation is not rewritten as wording in an
original written consent instrument. The unresolved institutional account is
carried separately from the methodological description.

## Historical cohort, versions and analytical denominators

The preserved historical analytical reference is 423 records. Retained aggregate
filter evidence describes a 490-to-423 flow, but that flow is neither a response
rate nor longitudinal attrition. Existing author-directed test/non-official
exclusions and coded restrictions are honored. Not every excluded record can
be identified as a test from the aggregate flow alone, and no excluded record is
reinstated or newly reclassified. No new academic dataset, estimation sample
or eligibility audit was produced for this package.

Five submitted form versions are distinguished explicitly. Differences concern
offered categories, available questions and routing, not just labels. For
residence, early code 4 denotes Other whereas later code 4 denotes Soacha;
Meta is an additional later option. The prepared representation preserves those
meanings rather than importing a uniform city code or pooling rare locations.
The municipality within Meta is not established by its department label.
Recorded geography is not automatically an interview-site identifier, and no
population-generalizing interpretation follows from inclusion of location terms.

Item and model denominators are question-specific. The reporting design retains
ordinary valid answers, structural skips, unavailable-in-version items/options,
applicable nonresponse, item-specific sentinel categories and unresolved
route/domain combinations. Nonresponse, an unasked item and an unoffered option
are not interchangeable. Nor do numeric codes 98 and 99 have a universal score.
Some represent substantively important uncertainty, refusal, pending or
not-applicable answers that must remain visible without entering a candidate
summary as zero.

Category shares use their declared recorded or ordinary-answer bases with the
relevant coverage counts alongside them. Multiple-response shares use the
specified parent denominator and may sum above 100%; a nonoffered option receives
no invented zero. Empty denominators leave percentages undefined. Semantic
contradictions are flagged for restricted review without changing answers or
adding exclusions. Model-specific complete valid inputs, candidate contributors
and the full historical cohort must be reported separately; there is no adopted
single complete-case cohort for the whole research.

## Outcomes and measurement architecture

Two engagement outcomes organize the academic analysis. The remittance outcome
retains the five q6_13 disposition categories and the accepted optional binary
distinguishing all-cash withdrawal from any reported digital retention or use.
It is not a measured monetary fraction, calibrated transaction intensity or the
channel of the latest remittance. The personal wording of this question remains
an applicability limitation within a household-remittance-eligible cohort.
The domestic outcome is the q6_14 yes/no report of digital participation in the
previous 60 days, with refusal and nonresponse unscored. Its operation-frequency
bins are described only among affirmative users, retaining uncertainty rather
than reconstructing transaction counts. Receipt frequency, latest channel and
domestic participation answer different questions.

Three provisional focal predictors are nominated. IAT3 averages the declared
phone-access, Internet-frequency and data/credit-runout components with fixed
one-third weights. IADT2 averages reported phone-transfer capability and
fraud-avoidance confidence, while a separate three-item comparison includes
smartphone-only application installation. IAFF2 averages two specific reported
administrative conditions: own-name proof of address and a financial-contact
telephone registered in the respondent's name. IAFF2's possible values are 0,
.5 and 1; it is not comprehensive formal financial inclusion or verified legal
eligibility. The exact component mappings remain in the separately preserved
preparation source and accepted measurement account.

These summaries require all declared scorable components. A no-phone answer is
valid phone-access information, but a phone-gated runout item can be structurally
unasked; the full IAT candidate is then unavailable rather than automatically
zero. Similarly, reported phone-transfer capability has its own applicability.
A model requiring complete focal candidates may consequently describe a narrower
subgroup than the study cohort. That coverage loss is a substantive result to
report, not a reason to fill missing scores, exclude women from the research
record or generalize the fitted association to all women. The runout item's
financial-use content also creates potential conceptual overlap with engagement.

The broader framework is retained through item-level and declared candidate
reports. It includes socioeconomic circumstances and calendar migration history;
reported OTP/PIN capability; selected financial activity; registration ease and
initial support; transaction experiences; prevention practices and perceptions;
adverse experiences and conditional redress; agency-related reports and household
arrangements; trust, social attitudes and anticipated consequences; and enabling
conditions, training/support and barriers. No new pooled IURD, IETR, IPCS, IEDF,
IAER, ICPF, IEH or IBPD score is created. Ordinal responses do not acquire
equal-interval meaning merely because they are coded numerically. Shared items,
including those discussed under IEH/IBPD, are not independent corroboration.

## Descriptive and association sequence

The first stage describes the cohort and the two engagement outcomes, preserving
their original categories and own denominators. The supplement records the
version-aware item distributions, candidate/component coverage and declared
definition comparisons across all fifteen domains. Small cells and rare
combinations require derivative-specific review before publication; restricted
counts are not automatically public-ready tables.

The second stage examines appropriate pairwise relationships and measurement
comparisons only under an explicit representation and denominator. Nominal
categories are not silently transformed into ordered scores, and the historical
15-field Pearson appendix is not adopted as a new mixed-domain correlation
strategy. Definition comparisons distinguish scoring changes from changes in
coverage and show common observations as well as own-N summaries. No new
correlation result or numerical tolerance is supplied here. Historical Appendix
C2 remains a held replication/comparison object, separate from academic choices.

## Conditional multivariable analysis and uncertainty

For each primary binary outcome, the accepted conditional model nominates a
logit link (StataCorp, n.d.-a) with IAT3, IADT2 and IAFF2, continuous age, categorical education and
version-aware residence. A corresponding extension adds nominal occupation and
first-arrival calendar history once its source, representation and support are
resolved. Completed secondary education, Bogotá and employee/worker are nominated
reference labels, conditional on actual support in the relevant model sample.
No occupation category establishes employment formality, and calendar first
arrival is not automatically continuous residence or time since latest return.

The four nominations are not a search grid or executed set of models. Before
fitting, the exact outcome variation, category support, valid joint inputs and
representation must be verified. Sparse support, collinearity, separation and
estimator omissions must be documented rather than repaired through unapproved
pooling, exclusion or model substitution. Changes between core and extension
results must distinguish covariate changes from changes in the contributing
sample; any common-sample refit must be explicitly included in a later manifest.

Reader-facing summaries will emphasize adjusted predicted probabilities and
interpretable probability differences, averaging over the stated model sample's
covariates, rather than a hypothetical mean respondent. Stata's margins framework
supports such prediction-based summaries (StataCorp, n.d.-b); the software
capability does not establish the suitability of these data. Exact score contrasts
must be fixed on substantive and support grounds, not selected after observing
favourable findings. A .1 IAFF change is not an observed administrative transition.
All probability summaries remain conditional associations, not intervention effects.

The variance/dependence approach remains unresolved. Generic heteroskedasticity-
robust errors do not by themselves address dependence induced by leader networks,
referrals or possible shared households. No city/enumerator cluster, survey
weight or missing network/household identifier is assumed. Pointwise 95% intervals
are a conditional reporting target after adoption of defensible uncertainty;
no interval or p-value is calculated in this package.

The accepted primary family consists of six overall conditional association
tests: the three focal constructs across the two main outcomes. Holm step-down
adjustment at nominal family-wise 5% is the conditional multiplicity policy
(Holm, 1979). Unadjusted p-values, adjusted p-values and pointwise intervals are
distinct objects. Nonestimable planned comparisons remain visible; the family
will not silently shrink, and its unavailable-test implementation must be fixed
before execution. Extensions and definition comparisons assess stability rather
than identify a preferred specification by significance. Measurement validity
is not decided by a p-value, and an imprecise or nonsignificant result is not
evidence that an association is absent.

## Conditional profiling and mixed-evidence integration

LCA is retained as a possible complementary strand, not a requirement for the
primary engagement analysis. The historical model grid, preferred-final state,
diagnostics, nonconvergence and numerical discrepancies remain preserved.
The historical four-class H1 solution is not automatically selected for the
academic paper. In particular, its IURD/IEDF inputs cannot be reconstructed by
renaming accepted item-level accounts that supply no replacement scalar indices.

A future profiling proposal must specify a coherent purpose, indicators,
representations, population, missingness rules and shared-content structure,
followed by a finite optimization, enumeration and stability plan. Fit statistics
must be interpreted within an appropriate comparable model space. No single
entropy threshold or preferred class count is adopted here. Class labels would
describe model-dependent response patterns rather than verified natural types
or permanent categories of women.

Any downstream class comparison must address classification uncertainty rather
than treat modal assignments as error-free. Vermunt (2010) provides relevant
methodological motivation for correcting naive three-step associations; it does
not establish compatibility of a particular correction with this project's
eventual mixed-input recipe. No BCH/three-step/one-step method is selected by
this citation. Items used to construct classes, or sharing the same content,
cannot be described as independent external validation. A defensible outcome
may be qualified descriptive profiles, a revised bounded model, inconclusive
profiling or no academic LCA.

Integration with the existing qualitative and institutional strands will compare
the scope and meaning of evidence, not force one-to-one correspondence between
statistical classes and qualitative positions. The coauthor's qualitative themes,
quotations, evidence and interpretations remain unchanged. Quantitative patterns
may motivate or qualify a discussion but cannot establish an unobserved causal
mechanism or rewrite qualitative accounts to match a preferred segmentation.
The exhibit plan retains all substantive domains across the article and technical
supplement while making the principal engagement findings legible.

## Reproducibility, provenance and limitations

Historical, style-only and academic source tracks are preserved separately.
Revisions are retrospective and are not described as original outcome-blind
preregistration. Exact source/version/input/operation/output manifests must
precede any separately authorized execution. All statistical execution, if
authorized later, uses the approved Stata MCP route. No Python/R estimates or
shell-launched Stata are substituted. The disabled master neither loads a
participant file nor bypasses the individual entry holds of its callees.

The present package supplies no new numerical result, score, fitted model,
posterior assignment or public dataset. Its main limitations remain the
non-probability recruited frame, incomplete recruitment/dependence information,
self-report and common-method content, instrument-version and routing
differences, unresolved clock provenance, model-specific coverage and conditional
measurement/model adoption. Institutional/consent, derivative disclosure,
coauthor/CFI review and final release conditions remain separate. These limits
must accompany eventual interpretation, not disappear when code runs.
