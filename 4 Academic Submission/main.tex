\section{Research foundation and analytical scope}
The academic paper will examine digital public infrastructure, financial
inclusion, and the experiences of migrant women in Colombia, drawing on the
project's existing quantitative, qualitative, institutional, and regional
materials. Four quantitative components must be reconciled to their original
inputs and outputs: descriptive analysis, correlations, regression models, and
latent class profiling. Migrant interviews, expert interviews, and regional
benchmarking must be located and represented faithfully, with their distinct
evidentiary roles retained.

This is a redevelopment of research whose estimates and interpretations have
already been seen. A later revision register cannot become a retrospective
claim of prospective preregistration. Historical results, revised exploratory
analyses, and genuinely future validation must remain distinguishable.

The literature and quantitative components are open to justified reassessment
after the baseline audit. The qualitative contribution is protected: this
preparation does not authorize recoding interviews, changing quotations or
themes, revising expert ratings, or altering substantive qualitative conclusions.
Integration requiring such changes must return to the authors for review.
