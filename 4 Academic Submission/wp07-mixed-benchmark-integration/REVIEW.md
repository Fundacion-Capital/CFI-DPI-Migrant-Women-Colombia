## What changed in the draft

1. **Method and inference.** The shared-base remittance specification is a separate refit, not a re-averaging of the original fit. The model-status Ns, descriptive Ns and fixed-six coefficient tests now have distinct jobs. The draft describes the working covariance assumption without suggesting that it corrects dependence from the recruitment process. An unavailable result keeps its slot, with a reason, rather than becoming zero.
2. **Substantive claim.** The paper leads with the observed qualitative contrast: using a domestic wallet and completing a transfer across borders are different things. It preserves examples of domestic work and trade, assisted use, differences across corridors and more independent users who still encounter constraints. Accounts of sending abroad cannot validate the survey’s estimates about handling received remittances. The policy discussion separates findings from outcomes a future evaluation would have to measure.
3. **Reader and exhibit design.** Two survey outcomes get separate bases, titles and notes. The benchmark compares institutions and operating arrangements rather than five national populations or a score. Planned exhibits are named as such. No numerical abstract, results section or final conclusion is ready for submission; they need adopted results. No quotations, charts or empirical counts were taken from the CFI summary to fill these gaps.

**Replacement captions for the eventual exhibits**

- **F1 — Reported disposition of remittance proceeds and domestic digital participation.** Panel A shows respondents’ choice among five substantive answers on the general disposition of remittances received. Panel B shows whether respondents report making or receiving a domestic digital payment or transfer in the previous 60 days, among valid yes/no answers. Each panel states its own valid N. Percentages are shares of respondents with those answers, not shares of remittance money. The panels do not follow the same transfer.
- **F2 (QF1) — Adjusted differences in reported digital engagement.** Points compare fitted probabilities at the higher and lower stated values, averaged over the corresponding core logit model’s estimation sample with other covariates at their observed values. The values are 0.50 and 0.75 for access/basic use and reported transfer ability/online-safety confidence; 0.50 and 1.00 for the two onboarding conditions. Units are percentage points. Bars give pointwise 95% delta-method intervals using the working robust covariance for independent records. The differences are conditional associations, not effects of increasing a score. See QT2 for the separate coefficient tests.
