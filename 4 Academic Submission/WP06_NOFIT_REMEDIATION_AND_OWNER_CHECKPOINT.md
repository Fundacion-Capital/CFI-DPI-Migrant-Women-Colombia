## 1. Concrete fail-closed remediation

The previous packet distinguished timeout from success but used a receipt-level quiescence label after registry removal. A successor must not generalize that label to proven engine termination. Its `safety_status` now represents registry absence, owned-command completion and engine-stop proof separately. A timed-out slot is quarantined even if the session is absent; late completion changes its descriptive status to a consumed timeout, never to a reusable slot. Every branch keeps `advance_allowed=false` and `retry_allowed=false`. The guard has no estimator dispatcher, participant reader, process killer or approval engine.

Independent process-exit evidence is evaluated separately. The implemented check requires a positively bound original PID/creation-time observation and two later OS observations showing that same PID absent. Registry metadata alone cannot satisfy it. Process exit proves eventual termination of that observed process, not timely cancellation of its command, adequacy of a production watchdog or authority to resume the analytical pipeline. This separation is deliberate: repairing a safety implication must not manufacture a capability the interface has not demonstrated.

The new guard is a standalone standard-library metadata module. Its tests exercise actual branches and mutations rather than a mocked estimator: registry-absence ambiguity, late completion, missing/wrong ownership/evidence, altered module snapshots, incomplete OS absence proof and attempted approval promotion. It cannot make a hypothetical response or a filesystem hash into an execution grant.
