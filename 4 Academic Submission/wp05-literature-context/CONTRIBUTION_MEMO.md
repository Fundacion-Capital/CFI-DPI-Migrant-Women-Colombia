# Contribution memo — From remittance receipt to usable digital value

**Recommendation.** Lead with the gap between *receiving money digitally* and *being able to use or control it digitally afterward*. Colombia’s early Bre-B period makes the distinction consequential: domestic interoperability can expand the possible uses of a balance without ensuring that an incoming remittance arrives digitally, that its intended recipient can transact independently, or that keeping value digital makes sense in her circumstances. Make this a mixed-methods paper about the conditions under which financial access becomes usable, not an evaluation of Bre-B or a general claim about women's empowerment.

## What the paper adds

Existing work already establishes that migrant status and documentation can matter for financial access, that digital wallets may be an entry point in Colombia, and that remittance practices are social and financial rather than purely technical. The paper can add a **recipient-side account of the steps between access, receipt and use**: how a household receives cross-border transfers; whether women take part in domestic digital transactions; and which reported frictions, safety experiences and household arrangements coexist with these patterns. The distinct evidence is the combination of these measures, in a defined group of women with international migration histories and in a documented institutional setting, with migrant and expert accounts helping interpret—not identify—the relevant mechanisms.

**Proposed framing, subject to final evidence:** “We study how migrant women in Colombia move from access to financial services and household remittance receipt to domestic digital use. We distinguish receipt channel from later use and examine how those behaviors coexist with reported transaction frictions, protection and household decision-making. We situate the study in the early operation of Colombia’s interoperable domestic payment system; the study does not estimate the effect of that rollout.”

## Competing explanations and evidence to retain

| Possible explanation | What would make it more plausible in the existing study | What cannot be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| Usable infrastructure: acceptance, fees, cash-out and available products | Separate descriptive distributions of receipt channel and domestic participation, with scoped transaction-experience and access items; preserve nonuse, non-applicability and uncertainty as appropriate. | A launch effect, a specific observed rail, an audited fee or an estimated counterfactual. |
| Selection and capacity: resources, documentation, literacy, phone access and networks | Clearly bounded sample and denominator notes; pre-specified descriptive contrasts, then only approved associative checks. Contextualize with relevant survey and qualitative evidence where present. | National prevalence, causal barriers or design-based representativeness; referral chains were not recorded. |
| Household obligations and practical value of cash | Preserve respondent versus household unit, separate reported decision arrangements from channel/use, and retain qualitative accounts that describe cash preference or shared practice where those accounts actually occur. | That cash use is exclusion, shared arrangements are coercion, or all flows are personally controlled. |
| Trust, risk and protection | Distinguish perceived safety, reported adverse experiences, complaint and conditional resolution, plus the actors described in the retained qualitative evidence. Show discordance as well as convergence. | Verified fraud/loss severity, verified protective behavior, or a single ranked protection or empowerment outcome. |
| Time, reverse direction and common causes | Label each question's actual recall/route; compare quantities only where the denominator and reference period match. Institutional timeline provides context but no treatment contrast. | A mediation result, causal ordering, or a transition probability from one stage to the next in the absence of longitudinal transaction observations. |

## Manuscript handoff

1. Open with the recipient-side question and the Colombian evidence it extends. Put the early Bre-B timing in the setting, not in the causal verb. Use the verified chronology to write a focused context section: documentation and onboarding, provider infrastructure, then domestic interoperability versus cross-border transfers.
2. Report the historically preserved study population and fieldwork route using the accepted D1 account. Clarify that the respondent is a woman and eligibility concerns household remittance receipt; the observational unit is not a verified unique household. Use no formal RDS weights or nationally representative language. Separate exact sample flow from response rate; honor historical manual exclusions.
3. Use item/domain labels and denominators from accepted D2/D3 and the separately adopted D4/D6 contract. Report descriptives first, correlations as co-movement, and approved regressions as adjusted associations. Treat candidate profiles as conditional/exploratory: preferred historical replay is not scientific clearance of a class solution, and revised item families cannot be substituted for historical indices by name. Keep any approved profiling in a clearly labeled place and omit academic class claims if the scientific gate remains unresolved.
4. Bring migrant qualitative accounts into interpretation before expert views and regional benchmarking. Preserve the coauthor's qualitative methods and evidence; do not re-code it in this work. Use experts for institutional interpretation and benchmarks for comparison, not as replication of respondent evidence or new causal validation.
5. In the discussion, tie each policy implication to the specific friction or trade-off the paper can document. Validate every final quantitative claim against the adopted exhibit, denominator and version. Finalize APA author/year metadata and primary institutional references at WP09–10; keep the legacy list intact.

**Suggested emphasis for a multidisciplinary development journal:** An account of what interoperable payments can make possible, and why that possibility is not the same as actual use or agency. The title, abstract and cover letter should not claim priority over all prior literature, a quantified rollout effect, or a redesign of the qualitative study. Institutional and release decisions remain separate.
