## 2. Historical procedure reported by the author

### 2.1 Administration and agreement to participate

The author confirms that trained migrant-women leader-enumerators read and explained the consent information before beginning each survey and recorded agreement to participate. Interviews were enumerator-assisted and conducted in Spanish. Participation was voluntary, did not affect access to programs or benefits, and involved no payment to respondents. Enumerator compensation was separate from respondent participation.

The author describes mixed telephone and in-person administration, with telephone interviews predominating. The reported documentation comprised a SurveyCTO consent response, additionally signed physical forms for in-person interviews, and recorded explicit consent for telephone interviews. This account does not establish whether every telephone consent was retained as an audio recording, where it is stored, or whether the public-sharing explanation is captured in it. A record-level completeness review was not performed. The unavailable or unretained mode-tracking sheet prevents verification of mode counts; all interviews must not be relabeled as telephone interviews.

### 2.2 Spoken public-sharing explanation

The author explicitly confirms that every respondent was told, before the survey began and as part of the standard consent explanation, that an anonymized version of her individual survey responses could be made publicly downloadable for other researchers. The author subsequently clarified that this public-download explanation was **spoken only**. No exact contemporaneous wording has been supplied or independently recovered.

The author describes the explanation as part of the standard process, not an explanation given only to selected respondents. This does not establish that a separately recorded public-sharing choice existed, that every respondent understood a particular licence or the unrestricted audience of an open repository, or that a general participation-consent field separately records agreement to public microdata release.

The author confirms that no respondent has requested withdrawal, deletion, exclusion from sharing or restriction of further research use, as reported in this conversation. No withdrawal register or request-handling system was audited. Do not convert this confirmation into an independently verified zero-request count or a permanent statement about future requests.

## 6. What must not be inferred or repaired retrospectively

- Do not backdate, replace or improve an original form and present the revised wording as administered historically.
- A currently dated author account may describe reported historical practice; it cannot manufacture exact past wording or missing participant decisions.
- Drafting a future information sheet/addendum is not obtaining consent. Any later participant contact or consent exercise needs its own institutional determination and authorization; none is initiated here.
- Do not decide automatically that re-consent is mandatory, unnecessary or sufficient. The institution must assess the existing basis and proposed use. A later review cannot be represented as pre-fieldwork approval.
- Consent to research, publication of findings, public microdata sharing, secondary research, quotations and identifiable media are distinct uses. Do not silently transfer permission between them.
- Removing direct identifiers does not establish anonymization. Assess linkage, rare combinations, free text, geography, metadata and released outputs before selecting open versus controlled access.
- Preserve the original dataset, manual exclusions, source code, historical fits and all prior packets. This account authorizes no participant load/export, fit, reclassification, new exclusion, contact, submission, push or release.
