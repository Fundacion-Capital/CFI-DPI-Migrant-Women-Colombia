## 1. Population and scope of claims

The author-confirmed recruitment population comprised adult women with **international migration experience**, including both women who had migrated to Colombia from another country and Colombian women who had lived abroad and returned.

International migration experience was required; internal migration or displacement alone did not qualify. The author reports that internally displaced women were not included.

The intended minimum residence period was approximately three months **following arrival or return to Colombia**. Earlier residence in Colombia did not, by itself, satisfy the post-return period for a Colombian return migrant.

Previous participation in Fundación Capital programs, personal phone ownership, and reading or writing ability were not participation requirements. Recruitment covered urban and peri-urban areas, not rural areas.

The author has expressly chosen the following scope of interpretation:

> Quantitative findings describe the recruited urban and peri-urban cohort of international migrant and Colombian return-migrant women. Broader implications may be discussed cautiously, but the estimates are not presented as statistically representative of all migrant women in Colombia.

This scope decision does not itself certify the eligibility of every historical record or authorize changes to the analysis.

## 2. Author-confirmed fieldwork account

The following describes implementation as confirmed by the author during this review. These confirmations must remain distinguishable from independently inspected records.

### Recruitment mechanism and interviewer selection

Fundación Capital selected a fixed team of migrant women leaders and previous program participants through existing relationships, recommendations and invitations. Demonstrated leadership and experience coordinating local activities were important considerations. Their community networks were an explicit reason for recruiting them as enumerators.

The trained leader-enumerators approached women they knew and reached additional women through respondent referrals. These same enumerators administered the interviews; referred respondents did not become interviewers merely by making referrals.

All quantitative respondents were reached through contacts or referrals. No additional sourcing methods were used. Women who had never participated in Fundación Capital programs were included.

No respondent-to-respondent referral links or recruitment-network structure were systematically recorded. The process should therefore be described as **non-probability, community-based snowball or chain-referral recruitment**, not as a formal respondent-driven sampling design.

### Interview targets and participant diversity

Each enumerator initially had a target of approximately 20 interviews. Some completed more to compensate for shortfalls among others and in response to the payment arrangement.

Enumerators received explicit instructions on distributing respondents across characteristics within their respective cities. The author recalls age and employment status among those characteristics. Instructions were provided through a written table, training sessions and continuing WhatsApp coordination.

The detailed allocation table is unavailable in the project folders. Accordingly:

- The account may describe instructed distributions and an effort to obtain heterogeneity.
- It may use descriptive tables to show the characteristics actually present in the analytical sample.
- It must not reconstruct numerical targets from those tables, claim verified quota attainment, or assert proportional representation against population benchmarks.

Older women and recent arrivals were reportedly harder to recruit. The extent of any resulting underrepresentation has not been quantified.

### Eligibility screening

Enumerators relied on respondents' answers. No official documentation was requested or checked before interviews.

The author confirms that enumerators followed the SurveyCTO screening questions and closed interviews when the form indicated ineligibility. Community knowledge facilitated recruitment but should not be described as independent documentary verification of eligibility.

### Administration and consent

All quantitative interviews were enumerator-assisted and conducted in Spanish. Respondents did not need to read or write.

Collection was mixed-mode. Enumerators chose telephone or in-person interviewing according to respondents' availability, sometimes rescheduling. The author reports that telephone interviews predominated.

Mode was recorded in a separate tracking sheet, not a SurveyCTO question. The sheet may not have been retained, and no numerical mode breakdown has been verified. The account must not substitute "all interviews were by telephone" for the confirmed mixed-mode procedure.

Enumerators read and explained consent information before recording agreement. The author confirms that:

- Consent was recorded through the SurveyCTO response.
- Physical consent forms were additionally signed for in-person interviews.
- Explicit consent was recorded for telephone interviews.
- Participation was voluntary and did not affect access to programs, services, assistance or benefits.

Respondents received no payment, reimbursement or other material benefit. Only enumerators were paid for their work.

These statements describe the reported quantitative procedure. They do not establish institutional approval or independently verify every individual consent record.

### Administrative validation and repeated participation

The author confirms that coordinators and core staff compared names and telephone numbers across all enumerators and cities to prevent the same woman from participating more than once.

Payments were based on unique women interviewed. The author also confirms that the administratively validated data were used for analysis, rather than a separate unreconciled export.

These are person-level administrative safeguards. They have not been independently reproduced through identifier matching in this review and must not be equated with the code's submission-ID checks.

### Household participation

Enumerators were instructed to recruit one woman per household, but this was not a strict eligibility restriction. Two different, otherwise eligible women from one household could participate.

Household links were not systematically recorded. Household overlap is a possibility that cannot now be established—not a documented occurrence.

The observational unit is therefore an individual woman. The historical record count must not be presented as a verified count of distinct households.

### Timing and closure

Recruitment ended through a combination of circumstances, primarily the deadline, with recruitment objectives, budget and available recruitment opportunities also contributing.

The author confirms that the early December interviews were actual fieldwork, not pilot or training exercises. The last recorded survey date and the formal administrative closure date remain different concepts.

### Programmed screening

The five recovered historical definitions use the same calculated eligibility conditions:

- Affirmative consent.
- Gender coded as "Mujer."
- Age at least 18; the age-entry constraint permits 18–100.
- Affirmative receipt of remittances by the respondent **or someone in her household** during the previous 12 months.

Nationality, arrival year and principal remittance receipt/management are not included in that calculation. The arrival-year constraint permits 1950–2026; it does not establish a five-year migration cutoff.

The ineligibility note refers to a principal recipient, while the calculation uses household receipt. Those formulations are not equivalent.
