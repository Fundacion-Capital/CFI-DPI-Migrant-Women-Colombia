\section{Mixed evidence and institutional comparison}
\subsection{Provenance of the qualitative evidence}
The manuscript re-examines the written migrant analysis and thematic memo, the Colombian institutional and regional expert memos, and the combined report prepared for the project. The original study used semistructured interviews and focus groups; the current paper does not recode transcripts. Written accounts can illustrate a reported arrangement. Repeated material in a report and a memo does not provide two observations. A guide shows how a question was meant to be asked, not which person heard it or how they answered it. The expert memos report perspectives on identity checks, provider operations, payment design and coordination; they do not establish a shared basis of direct observation of the sampled women.

The fieldwork archive and revised CFI report record 33 migrant interviews in five sites. The retained thematic synthesis reports 30; the surviving documentation does not establish whether the three Villavicencio interviews entered that synthesis. The focus-group record reports six sessions and 47 participations; the interview and group figures do not establish the number of distinct people across activities. Neither guide coverage nor the presence of a file establishes the basis of a given theme. Dates, recruitment route and individual mode of elicitation cannot be assigned to the themes below when the written source does not preserve them. We do not claim saturation.

To compare sources, first match the units, direction, stage, time period and kind of observation. When sources address different stages they can complement each other without corroborating a particular result \citep{ocathainMurphyNicholl2010,guettermanFettersCreswell2015}. Learning through use, earlier skills and help from other people remain plausible even when the adjusted use contrast is positive. The sources would need a common time order or a different comparison to distinguish them \citep{humphreysJacobs2023}. The analysis links neither cases nor likelihoods and makes no causal estimate.

\subsection{Units of institutional comparison}
The five cases are those of the original CFI comparison. They illustrate differences in public settlement and rules, connections between existing services, and retail interfaces. They were not chosen to estimate a country effect. The DPI Map citation refers to the historical version used in that comparison \citep{institute_for_innovation_and_public_purpose_ucl_dpi_2025}. The February 2026 Colombian document and the first-half 2025 Peru report are dated snapshots; the undated Brazil, Costa Rica and Mexico operator descriptions were consulted on 19 November 2026. Requirements below describe the service or interface, not the documents a specific migrant must show. An unfilled comparison is not evidence that a provision does not exist.

\section{Consent, institutional review and data availability}
\paragraph{Quantitative survey.} The project team's documentary account records that enumerators explained participation and recorded agreement in the SurveyCTO consent field before substantive questions. The retained instrument documents that field. The team reports an additional oral explanation of possible sharing of anonymized survey responses; the written participation item did not separately record agreement to public downloading. This account distinguishes the available written and oral evidence; it does not assert that a public participant-level file is authorized.

\paragraph{Interviews and focus groups.} The original qualitative strand used a separate written participation process. The authors report retaining signed qualitative participation documents. This revision uses the existing written thematic analysis, not participant consent files, recordings or newly recoded transcripts. It makes no claim about how specific optional permissions were exercised or about authorization to release qualitative records.

\paragraph{Review and funding.} The project team reports that Fundación Capital reviewed the research protocol internally and gave oral authorization before fieldwork. No external university or committee approval or exemption is claimed. The Center for Financial Inclusion funded the study, and Fundación Capital administered the project and recruited the survey leaders. The manuscript reports the authors' interpretation; these statements are not approvals of the draft or package.

\paragraph{Availability.} Participant-level inputs and auxiliary outputs remain restricted, with no standing offer of external access. The paper and supplement report existing screened aggregates. Code, documentation and selected aggregate outputs would be considered separately for disclosure review before any release. This draft does not release a new package or promise access to records. The archival H1 and exploratory T02 sections document model comparisons; the main analysis adopts no latent class or respondent memberships.
