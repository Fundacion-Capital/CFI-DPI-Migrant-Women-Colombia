\section{Mixed evidence and institutional comparison}
\subsection{Provenance of the qualitative evidence}
The manuscript revisits the written migrant synthesis and memo, the Colombian institutional and regional expert memos, the integrated institutional report and the account prepared for CFI. It uses the retained interview and focus-group guides to check what a question was designed to elicit. A guide is not proof a question was asked, and a hypothetical exercise cannot establish behavior. Repeating the same account in a memo, report or presentation does not create independent evidence. We do not recode interviews, calculate the frequency of a theme, reinterpret an unnamed speaker's contribution as group consensus, or treat later written summaries as new fieldwork.

The fieldwork archive and revised report record 33 migrant interviews in five sites. The retained thematic synthesis states 30. The archive contains three Villavicencio interview documents, but the available coding documentation does not establish their historical inclusion. The focus-group record reports six sessions with 47 participants. Additional files or versions in the archive do not establish another session. The interview and group totals are not a count of unique individuals across activities. Neither the archive count nor the guides establish whom the earlier analysis covered for a given theme. We do not claim analytic saturation or identify individual participants across sources.

\subsection{How to read the joint display}
For each row, compare who or what was observed, the reporting period, the direction and stage of the transaction, and how the claim was elicited. When two sources describe different steps, they may give a fuller account together without verifying each other's results. When the question was not asked or the account does not document the step, that silence is not evidence that an experience did not occur \citep{ocathainMurphyNicholl2010,guettermanFettersCreswell2015}.

Three examples illustrate the limits. First, accounts of sending toward Venezuela can show why a local transfer is not the same as delivery abroad. They cannot explain the survey frequency of digital retention or use of incoming proceeds. Second, institutional comments on verification help distinguish holding a document from a provider accepting it, without verifying an application or a model contrast. Third, the group guide's imagined Bre-B transfer can elicit expectations but cannot establish platform usage. We preserve competing readings when the record cannot distinguish them: prior experience may support use, use may build confidence, and third-party help may make both possible \citep{humphreysJacobs2023}. We integrate neither individuals nor likelihoods and draw no causal estimate. The four overlapping positions in the migrant synthesis and the four observed survey configurations are different objects; neither validates a fitted class.

\subsection{Units of institutional comparison}
\paragraph{Sources and units.} The February 2026 Banco de la República technical report documents Bre-B as a domestic immediate-payments scheme during the survey period \citep{banreptechnical2026}. The Banco Central de Reserva del Perú strategy report for the first half of 2025 describes the phased linkage of existing wallets, beginning with Yape and Plin \citep{bcrp2025}. The undated operator descriptions consulted on 19 November 2026 cover distinct units: the Banco Central do Brasil's Pix scheme, settlement platform and alias directory \citep{bcbpix}; the Banco Central de Costa Rica's SINPE Móvil retail service connecting mobile numbers to colón accounts \citep{bccrsinpe}; and Banco de México's SPEI interbank transfer rail \citep{banxicospei} and separate CoDi payment-initiation interface \citep{banxicocodi}. The original DPI Map citation refers to the historical version used in the benchmark, not the current live release \citep{institute_for_innovation_and_public_purpose_ucl_dpi_2025}.

The dated documents and service descriptions establish selected design features and institutional responsibilities. They do not supply comparable evidence on acceptance of migrant documents, usability, remittance delivery, realized costs, effective recourse, gender-disaggregated uptake or the quality of interagency coordination. Where those outcomes are not documented in comparable units, we leave them undetermined rather than assigning a score.
